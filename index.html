Science Calendar

An anniversary calendar for the history of science. Support and contact.

Science Calendar shows you what happened on today's date in the history of science — 419 events, with every one of the 366 days of the year covered, 29 February included. It can send you a daily note, and it can copy the whole lot into your own calendar as recurring yearly anniversaries.

Common questions

How do I turn on the daily notification?

Settings tab → On This Day Alerts, then pick a time. iOS will ask permission the first time. Days with nothing recorded stay silent, so you will not get an empty notification.

I re-ran the export and nothing was added. Why?

That is deliberate — it is how the app avoids creating duplicates. It keeps its own record of what it has already written, and skips those.

If you deleted the events in Calendar yourself, the app cannot tell. It asks for write-only calendar access, which means it can add events but is technically incapable of reading your calendar back — a narrower permission, kept narrow on purpose. Use Forget what was exported on the Export tab and run it again.

Why do fewer events export than the app contains?

Because not every date is known to the day. 404 of the 419 events have a real calendar day and become anniversaries; the other 15 are known only to a month, a year, or approximately. Rather than invent a day for them, the app keeps them as a browsable timeline and leaves them out of the export.

What does “approx.” mean on an event?

That the date is a reconstruction rather than a record. Fleming's penicillin mould is the clearest case: 28 September 1928 is the traditional date, his own later accounts disagree, and the entry says so. The app would rather tell you a date is shaky than present it as settled.

Some dates look wrong for the period.

Dates before the Gregorian changeover are given as the modern (proleptic Gregorian) value, with the Old Style date in the note — that is the day the anniversary actually falls on in the calendar your phone uses. Newton's Principia, for instance, is dated 5 July 1687 Old Style, and the entry tells you so.

Where do the dates come from?

Person entries cite the Wikidata item the date came from; event entries cite the Wikipedia article. Every citation was re-fetched and checked against the entry, which found and fixed real errors. It is corroboration against reference works rather than primary sources — it would not catch a mistake that Wikipedia and Wikidata share — so the sources are shown in the app for you to judge.

I found an error in an event.

Please report it. Include the event title and what you believe the correct date or detail is, with a source if you have one. Corrections are welcome and are the main way the dataset improves.
